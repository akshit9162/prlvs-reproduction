Research paper reproduction

The rank correlation doesn't reproduce, and the reward is why.

A from-scratch PyTorch reimplementation of Progressive Reinforcement Learning for Video Summarization (Wang, Wu & Yan, Information Sciences 2024), evaluated against all 20 TVSum annotators per video. Every decision the paper leaves unstated is recorded, and a set of controlled baselines locates the failure in the reward itself, not the RL optimiser.

What was built

Two policies editing a K-frame summary

PRLVS keeps an intermediate summary of K = 10 frames and improves it step by step. Each step, one policy picks which frame to move and a second picks how far. Both are A2C actor–critics on a shared LSTM encoder, trained in alternation (Alg. 1).

01FeaturesGoogLeNet pool5, 1024-d, 2 fps
02StateLSTM over the K selected frames
03Horizontal πWhich of K frames to move; information reward (Eq. 6–7)
04Vertical πMove −1 / +1 / −5 / +5; diversity + representativeness (Eq. 3–5)
05StopKeep-order termination (Eq. 15)
06ScoreSelection frequency over rollouts → per-frame score
What the paper doesn't say

Every unstated decision, written down

The paper can't be implemented from its text alone. PROVENANCE.md tags every constant as coming from the paper, from the reference reproduction, or decided here.

Eq. 4 is degenerate as printed

Its minimum ranges over the selected frames themselves, so it is 0 at i = j. Implemented as Zhou et al.'s representativeness, which the paper says it follows.

Eq. 13 is constant as printed

λ = ⌊E_t/E_c⌋ mod 2, with E_t the total number of iterations, never alternates. Read as the current iteration.

Eq. 6 has no dynamic range

A product of 10 probabilities is about 1e-10, so the reward difference is about 1e-15. The repaired variant computes it in the log domain.

Unstated constants

γ, horizon T, sampling rate and F1 segmentation are all missing from the paper, so each one is recorded with its source.

What it means

The objective, not the optimiser

The pipeline isn't the bottleneck. A selector that knows the human scores reaches τ = 0.73 through exactly the same scoring.
The reward doesn't track human importance. Frames that directly maximise the paper's reward score τ ≈ +0.01, no better than random, so an RL agent that optimises that reward perfectly still can't beat random on this metric.

This matches an independent reproduction, which reaches the paper's 63% F1 but τ = −0.011. Otani et al. (CVPR 2019) showed that random summaries score well under F1 on these benchmarks. That is why rank correlation was introduced, and it is the metric on which this method fails to reproduce.
